Track 1 · Bit’s growing corner

AI & Machine Learning

For projects where a model learns from data to make a prediction, classification, recommendation, or other decision. Projects might use computer vision, natural language processing, forecasting, or another machine-learning method. The model should be doing meaningful work in the final project, not added as an extra feature.

Example projects

  • A vision model that measures untouched food on cafeteria trays to reveal recurring waste patterns
  • Predict likely harvest timing using planting dates, weather conditions, and growing degree days
  • Recommend ingredient swaps based on what an ingredient actually does in a recipe, such as binding, acidity, moisture, or leavening

Track 2 · Amp’s workshop terrace

Hardware

Solve a food or agriculture problem with a physical device. Teams might use Arduino or Raspberry Pi boards, sensors, motors, cameras, or other electronic and mechanical components. The device should be the center of the project, even if it also connects to a dashboard or uses a model behind the scenes. Kitchen tools, farm systems, storage, and transportation projects can all fit here.

Example projects

  • A fruit-ripeness tester
  • A produce box that automatically vents itself when temperature and humidity raise the risk of spoilage
  • A sensor probe that signals when a compost pile needs water, airflow, or turning

Track 3 · Bite’s play patch

Game Design

For projects where the main experience is a game. Build around food, farming, sustainability, nutrition, access, or culture, but let gameplay drive the project rather than a webpage with points attached. Focus on the choices a player makes based on the rules of the world, and what changes as the game unfolds. Browser, mobile, multiplayer, and simulation games all fit here.

Example projects

  • A season-by-season strategy game where crop rotation changes soil, pest pressure, and profit
  • A restaurant puzzle where players turn a shrinking pantry into dishes before ingredients spoil
  • A multiplayer supply-chain game where farms, stores, and shoppers react to shortages and surprise events

Track 4 · The harvest ledger

Data Science

Use food or agriculture data to uncover patterns and turn them into something useful. The focus is on understanding what the data shows rather than predicting a new outcome. If the main feature predicts or classifies new inputs, it belongs in AI & Machine Learning.

Example projects

  • Map which cafeteria foods are most commonly left behind and when food waste spikes during lunch
  • Compare price, water use, emissions, and seasonality across possible ingredient substitutions
  • Visualize how distance, refrigeration, and time add cost between a farm and the grocery-store shelf
